## Framework: three guiding principles × three stages (from the cheat sheet, verbatim where short)

**Visualize** — show rather than tell. **Unify** — make everything consistent. **Focus** — direct attention.

| Stage / topic | Visualize | Unify | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| DESIGN: Theory | Organise your thoughts and plan in a process that works for you | Consider how the presentation holds together and how it differs from the written report | Move your conclusion to the front; hook the audience right away |
| DESIGN: Color | Colour matters — use it carefully and strategically to highlight | Be consistent with colours throughout | Use colour to draw attention to specific parts of slides |
| DESIGN: Type | Audience is influenced immediately by text; choose carefully | Select **one to two fonts** for the whole deck | Use **larger text** so the audience reads quickly and returns to listening |
| BUILD: Text | When possible convert text to visuals (people remember visuals) | Similar layouts and structure across text slides | Hierarchy + the **layering** technique to steer attention |
| BUILD: DataViz | Use preattentive attributes (colour, lines, shapes, markings) to show data | Link text and visuals; integrate legends into graphs | Remove nonessential gridlines, tick marks, data labels |
| BUILD: Image | Large, high-resolution images that fill the whole slide | Consistent use of images | Link text and image so they point at the important part |
| BUILD: Scaffolding | Large visuals / simple text as section markers | Similar colours, fonts, images, icons as guideposts | Scaffolding slides focus attention at transitions |
| GIVE: Presenting | Relaxation strategies for nerves | Pull content, delivery style and stories into one theme | Practice, practice, practice — dedicated rehearsal time |
| GIVE: Nitty gritty | Know the screen/projector and whether it is 4:3 or 16:9 | Handouts consistent with slides and talk | Know adapters/inputs and the room |

## Rules (concrete)

1. Plan before you open the slide software; fill the **Presentation Worksheet**: (1) type of presentation (small
   meeting, seminar, lecture, pitch, keynote, workshop); (2) audience (colleagues, managers, scientists, sales,
   students, mixed); (3) the **headline message**; (4) what you want the audience to do with your conclusions;
   (5) opening statement; (6) closing statement; (7) outline of up to 5 sections; (8) stories you can tell;
   (9) images to sketch before searching (graphs, pictures/icons, videos); (10) anticipated Q&A.
2. Structure = pyramid (intro, literature, data, methods, results, conclusions) + inverted pyramid (lede first) =
   **hourglass**: hook & preview → methods, data, details → conclusions & takeaways. Key results go at the start and end,
   where attention is highest; do not end on robustness tables.
3. Reject fixed slides-per-minute rules; split thoughts over more slides instead.
4. **Layering text**: one point per click under a concise, active title; previous points grey out.
5. **Layering graphs**: build a chart one series at a time.
6. Section divider slides ("scaffolding"): big number, highlighted stage box, or full-bleed photo.
7. Images: organise several images into a unified grid/collage; prefer full-screen photos with a text box; avoid
   small, low-quality or clichéd stock photos.
8. Text big: "your slides should look more like a billboard".
9. Use **sans serif** type (thicker, easier to see); establish hierarchy — main title large and bold, subtitle,
   body in a block.
10. Remove bullet markers; start items directly. Minimise text; slides support you, they are not a transcript.
11. Colour: cool colours (blue, green) recede, warm (red, yellow) pop; pick a deliberate palette; a light grey background
    instead of pure white (reviewer disliked the clash with white-background figures — make figure backgrounds match).
12. Tables on slides: drop standard errors, covariates, R² that belong in the paper; show the few numbers that matter.
13. Rehearse out loud, standing, with a clicker, until wording is natural and padding is cut.
14. Logistics checklist (Supplies Checklist): confirm audience type/number, room, times, A/V, breaks, who introduces,
    webinar platform, sharing slides and fonts, Q&A length; bring laptop, charger, adapters, clicker, batteries, USB
    backup, water; silence phone.

## Conflicts with other sources

- Schwabish allows (grey) text slides with layering; Alley/Doumont require a sentence message as title and no bullets;
  Schwabish asks only for a "concise, active title".
- "Fill the slide with a large image" conflicts with Tufte's preference for dense evidence and with Doumont's
  signal-to-noise (a decorative photo is noise unless it carries the message).
- Light-grey background vs Goldsmith-Pinkham's off-white/black and Alley's "dark on light or light on dark".
- Reviewer (Püttmann) notes Beamer is poor for annotating empirical figures compared with PowerPoint.
