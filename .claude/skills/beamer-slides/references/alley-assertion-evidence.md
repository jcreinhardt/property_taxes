# Michael Alley — Assertion-Evidence slide design / *The Craft of Scientific Presentations*

- Author: Michael Alley (Leonhard Center, Penn State). Book: *The Craft of Scientific Presentations: Critical Steps
  to Succeed and Critical Errors to Avoid*, 2nd ed., Springer, 2013 (full text read via a course mirror,
  https://uebungen.physik.uni-heidelberg.de/c/image/f/vorlesung/20251/2050/The_Craft_of_Scientific_Presentations.pdf).
- Site: https://www.assertion-evidence.com/ (read), tutorial https://craftscicom.org/ae_tutorial.html (read),
  templates https://craftscicom.org/ae_templates.html (read; `ae_presentation_template_16_9_ratio.pptx` and the 4:3
  version, no layout specs stated on the page), research https://www.assertion-evidence.com/research-papers.html (read).
- Origin: structure developed at Hughes Aircraft (1970s) and Lawrence Livermore National Lab (1980s).

## The structure

A slide = a **sentence-assertion headline** (the message: hypothesis, assumption, insight or result) + **visual
evidence** (photographs, drawings, diagrams, graphs, equations, short tables, films) with words only where
necessary. **Bulleted lists do not occur.** Secondary details go in the speech or the notes page. Slides are a
visual aid during the talk, not a handout, and serve the audience, not the speaker's notes.

## Rules (Tables 4-2, 4-3, 4-4 of the book, exact numbers)

1. Headline is a succinct complete sentence stating the slide's main assertion; **no more than two lines**.
2. **Left-justify** the headline; start it in the **upper-left corner** (where Western audiences look first).
3. Capitalise as a sentence (first word + proper nouns); the final period is optional (standalone sentence).
4. If the headline wraps, break the first line so phrases stay together; no orphan word on line 2; second line not much longer than the first.
5. **28-point** headline type (PowerPoint's default 44 pt is too large for a meaningful sentence in ≤ 2 lines).
6. Body text **18–24 pt** (24/20/18 depending on importance and space); reference listings **12–14 pt**, not bold.
7. Sans-serif typeface (Calibri recommended; Arial acceptable); **boldface** the type, especially in large rooms.
8. No italics, no underline, no ALL CAPS (a capital takes ~35 % more horizontal space; all three slow reading).
9. Dark type on light background or light on dark; avoid hot background colours (red, orange, yellow); no decorated backgrounds.
10. Logos only on the first and last projected slides (every slide is fine on a handout); if kept, put them in a
    bottom corner, never top-left; remove whenever they crowd the evidence.
11. Keep every text block, not only the headline, to ≤ 2 lines (focus groups: nearly everyone reads 1–2-line blocks,
    more than half will not read 3+ lines).
12. Keep any list or set of call-outs to **2, 3 or 4 items**; fold the rest into speech.
13. Project **no more than 20 words per minute** (assertion-evidence talks measured < 20 wpm vs 35–40 wpm for typical topic-subtopic decks).
14. Use animation only when it serves the audience; no "fly in" effects.
15. Leave enough blank space between elements that the slide breathes.
16. Title slide should orient (engaging image + title + names), mapping slide should map (images for the 2–4 parts,
    re-used as icons in the corner of later slides instead of a logo), concluding slide should emphasise the main
    results; short conference talks often need no outline slide.
17. Cite image sources on the slide as short reference listings ([Author, year]).
18. Rehearse; arrive early; prepare for Murphy's Law (fonts missing on the room's computer, projector failure).

## Evidence

- Garner, Alley, Wolfe & Sawarynski (2013, IJEE 29(6)): same narration, AE vs common-practice slides — AE audiences
  understood and remembered more (p < .01).
- Garner, Alley & Aippersbach (2016, IJEE 32(1A)): presenters who *built* AE slides understood their own content better.
- Alley, Zappe, Garner: "Projected words per minute" studies behind rule 13.

## Conflicts with other sources

- 28 pt/18–24 pt are PowerPoint point sizes. Beamer's 16:9 canvas is 160 mm × 90 mm; a PowerPoint 16:9 slide is
  13.33 in (338.7 mm) wide, so Beamer sizes scale by ~2.12 when both fill the screen: 11pt ≈ 23 pt, `\large` ≈ 30 pt,
  `\footnotesize` ≈ 19 pt. A Beamer 11pt body with a `\large` headline therefore meets Alley's sizes; too much text is the
  usual failure. (Corrected 2026-11-04; the first draft used the 4:3 width.)
- Calibri/Arial bold conflicts with Schwabish ("avoid Arial, Calibri") and with Goldsmith-Pinkham/Butts/metropolis (Lato, Libertine, Fira).
- "No bullets ever" is stricter than Goldsmith-Pinkham, Butts and Schwabish, who all keep (sparse) lists.
- Doumont agrees on sentence title (≤ 2 lines, ≤ ~12 words) and no logos; Tufte agrees on sentences over bullets but
  rejects the low-resolution one-message-per-slide format itself.
