## Concrete rules — the 22 tips

1. **Confirm the time slot** (a "75-minute" seminar may be 45 + 15 Q&A). **Rule of thumb:
   one slide per minute — a 15-minute conference talk has at most 15 slides.** A 75-minute
   seminar doesn't need 75; his seminar decks are usually under 60.
2. **Let the time determine the content.** The shorter the talk, the more concisely you
   must answer "Why should we care?" (his 15-minute CSAE talk: three motivation slides,
   boiled down to "FGM affects 100 million women").
3. **Practice under time limits** and time yourself, especially as a beginner. (He admits
   he never practises; "do not copy that".)
4. **Don't showcase technical skill** — most of the audience has done similar work.
   **Assume smart college graduates without field expertise**; focus on motivation,
   intuition, plain-English definitions.
5. **Translate technical material as you go** (theory, empirical framework,
   identification) into plain English, and bring the audience back to the big picture in
   the conclusions.
6. **Include an outline slide** (unless the Beamer theme shows section progress).
7. **Preview your results** — "a murder mystery in reverse": people enjoy the journey more
   when they know the destination.
8. **LaTeX/Beamer**: PDFs render identically everywhere; PowerPoint equations from a Mac
   break on a PC.
9. **Introduction = Keith Head's formula: Hook → Research question → Antecedents (the 4–5
   closest studies) → Value added → Roadmap.**
10. **Leave the literature review out of the slides** — "dull to read, worse to hear."
11. **Standard flow after the intro: theoretical framework → empirical framework → data →
    results → limitations → conclusion**, trimmed to time. **"Magic Appendix Trick": 15
    main slides followed by ~30 appendix slides** (descriptives, robustness, proofs, extra
    graphs) to pull up when asked.
12. **In an empirical talk, show only the model's assumptions and testable predictions**;
    full model in the appendix unless theory is the contribution.
13. **Mirror the paper's order and reuse its sentences** — rewriting is part of writing.
14. **Tell the story with one key graph** where possible; his best-received papers each
    have one graph that carries the story.
15. **Tables: only the coefficients of interest**, plus rows like "Control variables? Yes
    / Village fixed effects? Yes"; full tables in the appendix.
16. **Speak conversationally: don't read slides, don't memorise them.** Grad students:
    skip jokes, signal competence; humour comes later in a career.
17. Fieldwork photos only if necessary (smiling-children photos are a cliché).
18. Maps only when identification uses spatial variation; otherwise appendix.
19. **No "Questions?" / "Thank you!" closing slide** — people will ask anyway; thank them
    at the start or end verbally.
20. Posters: print locally in the conference town.
21. **Answer questions honestly**: say "I don't know"; flag off-the-cuff answers as such;
    questions of little general interest or far afield → "let's discuss after the talk".
22. **Enjoy it.** "Your enthusiasm is the most effective way to convey excitement about
    your research"; if you aren't having fun, neither is the audience.

## Doing Economics ch. 3 (third-party summary — unverified)

- Covers invited seminars ("the ideal format for presenting a paper" — learn departmental
  norms, structure the talk like the paper), job talks (more self-presentation and fit;
  balance technical detail with clarity), conference talks (shorter, focused, reduced
  slide content), online/hybrid (be more concise), lightning talks/posters, lay
  audiences, keynotes (broader themes, not the paper du jour — a point he also made on the
  blog).
- Reportedly adapts the one-slide-per-minute rule and suggests **3–4 bullets per slide**.
- Questions: take notes, restate the question, admit knowledge gaps.
- Discussants: constructive, summarise, actionable improvements, not typos.

## Applies to economics seminar / job talk?

Yes — the only source written for both the 15-minute conference slot and the 75-minute
seminar, with explicit slide counts and the Head introduction formula; applied/development
economics slant (regression tables, fieldwork, maps).

## Conflicts with common advice

- **~1 slide/minute** vs. Cochrane ("you will not be able to put up 1 slide per minute"),
  Li (2 min/slide), EUI (12 slides/hour).
- **Outline slide + preview** vs. Peyton Jones (no outline) and Cochrane (no preview).
- Includes **antecedents** in the intro while dropping the lit review — Peyton Jones and
  Cochrane drop related work entirely in talks.
- "Don't memorise" vs. Peyton Jones "script your first few sentences".
