## The five rules that recur most often

1. **Cut ruthlessly; present a few things well** (10/10).
2. **Aim at a smart non-expert; the audience knows far less than you think** (9/10).
3. **Practise aloud, timed, with other people** (8/10).
4. **Sparse slides, few words, never read them; slides support the voice** (8/10).
5. **No literature review in the talk** (8/10; two allow a brief "antecedents" line) —
   tied with **one take-away the audience must leave with** (7/10) and **hook within the
   first minutes** (7/10).

## Disagreements

1. **Preview the results?** Shapiro, Bellemare, EUI: yes (Bellemare: "murder mystery in
   reverse"). Tabarrok: optional. Cochrane: never — "why say it twice?" — and skip
   motivation too: "Look at table 1."
2. **Motivation length.** Peyton Jones: 2 minutes / 20% of the talk. Tabarrok and EUI:
   5 minutes. Winston: vision + "done something" inside 5 minutes. Li: reach the model
   within 3 of 20 minutes (7/45, 10/75). Cochrane: zero.
3. **Slides per minute.** Bellemare ≈ 1 slide/minute (≤15 for 15 min; <60 for 75).
   Cochrane: "you will not be able to put up 1 slide per minute". Li: 2 minutes per
   slide. EUI: 12 per hour (long slides count double).
4. **Outline slide.** Peyton Jones: "conveys near zero information" at the start (maybe
   after the motivation). Bellemare, EUI, Tabarrok, Fatahalian: include one / use section
   slides. Winston: verbal enumeration instead.
5. **Selling vs. teaching.** Meager: "you are in sales and/or marketing"; Peyton Jones:
   make them "foam at the mouth" to read the paper. Li: "teaching, not advocacy ... if
   you act like you are selling something, the audience will treat you with suspicion."
6. **Questions.** Meager: engage, try twice before deferring; Peyton Jones: invite them,
   truncate the talk rather than lose the connection; EUI: few questions is a bad sign.
   Tabarrok: "do not let the audience dominate you", deflect and return to the story;
   Cochrane: count to three before answering.
7. **Pausing.** Shapiro: "no pauses unless you really want to stress something." Li:
   pause on every equation, longer than you think; EUI: stop between slides, occasional
   long stops.
8. **Slide titles and text.** Fatahalian/Tabarrok: title = the point; Fatahalian: read
   the titles as the summary. Winston: remove titles, bullets and logos; 40–50 pt font.
   Cochrane: slides of only equations/tables/graphs. EUI: 24 pt, ≤15 lines.
9. **Ending.** Winston: final slide "Contributions", never "thank you". Fatahalian: end on
   a bright future-work note. Cochrane: strike "I leave x for future research". EUI: ends
   with future research after a ≤4-point take-home. Bellemare/Winston: no
   "Questions?/Thank you" slide.
10. **Memorise or not.** Peyton Jones: script the first sentences; Fatahalian: scripted
    transitions in speaker notes. Bellemare: don't memorise, stay conversational.
11. **Who to rehearse with.** Winston: never your advisor (they hallucinate content that
    isn't there); EUI/Tabarrok/Li: anyone, a friend, the home department.
12. **Repetition.** Winston: cycle, say it three times (20% fogged out at any moment).
    Cochrane: say it once, right.
13. **Software.** Bellemare, Tabarrok: Beamer. Li: PowerPoint/Keynote — "if Beamer is
    easier, you have too many equations."
